Canvas guide

13 vs 18 mesh needlepoint canvas

Mesh count controls the physical size of every stitch. It affects how much detail fits in the design, which threads cover the canvas, how easy the holes are to see, and how long the project may take.

Mesh means intersections per inchA higher number packs more stitches into each inch. It does not mean a larger hole or a larger finished design.

The short answer

Choose 13 mesh when you want larger holes, thicker thread, a bolder image, or a faster project. Choose 18 mesh when you want a smaller finished piece or more room for detail and do not mind smaller holes and more stitches.

How mesh changes finished size

A chart contains a fixed number of stitches. Divide those stitches by the mesh count to find the finished inches. Moving the same chart from 18 mesh to 13 mesh makes the design about 38 percent larger in each direction: 72 stitches ÷ 18 is 4 inches, while 72 ÷ 13 is about 5.5 inches.

That size change also affects finishing. A design intended for a 4-inch round ornament may no longer fit the same form when stitched on a lower mesh count. Use the mesh calculator before buying canvas or finishing supplies.

When 13 mesh is a good choice

  • You prefer larger holes or find 18 mesh tiring to see.
  • The design uses broad color areas and strong outlines.
  • You want to use a thicker decorative thread or wool.
  • The finished piece can be physically larger.
  • You are making a pillow, bold ornament, or first project where speed matters more than tiny details.

Because fewer stitches fit into the same number of inches, delicate features need to be simplified. Very small lettering, subtle eyes, and thin lines may need manual cleanup.

When 18 mesh is a good choice

  • You need more detail within a small finished area.
  • The photo has important facial features, lettering, or curved edges.
  • You want a compact ornament, insert, or framed miniature.
  • You are comfortable stitching smaller holes with finer thread.
  • You would rather preserve shape detail than finish quickly.

More available stitches do not guarantee a good result. A busy photo can still create scattered colors and hard-to-follow confetti. Crop and simplify the photo before using the extra resolution.

What about 10, 12, or 14 mesh?

Those are common choices too. The same formula applies: stitches divided by mesh equals inches. Needlepoint Maker currently offers 13 and 18 mesh because they are the two sizes most US stitchers buy. The standalone calculator supports 10, 12, 13, 14, and 18 mesh for existing charts.

Thread coverage comes after canvas choice

The thread must cover the canvas without becoming so thick that it distorts the intersections or so thin that the canvas shows through. Brand, fiber, stitch type, and personal tension all matter, so make a small test swatch on the actual canvas. Use the test to judge coverage and color together.

Practical rule: choose mesh from the finished size and level of detail first. Then test thread coverage on that canvas rather than choosing canvas only to match thread already on hand.

A decision you can make in two questions

  1. Must the piece fit a specific finished size? If yes, calculate how many stitches that size provides at each mesh.
  2. Does the subject need fine detail? If yes, use the higher mesh when the holes and stitching time are comfortable for you.
